## Session intent
User: Let's start by writing a function that reads the CSV data and groups by user_id. The dataset has ~2M rows across 15 columns and lives at /data/raw/events_2024_q3.csv. We need an aggregation that produces per-user click counts, session durations, and last-active timestamps.

## Constraints (safety / policy)
Before we ship anything, remember the data-handling policy: the events CSV contains user PII (email, IP, device fingerprint). All processing must comply with the company's privacy policy v3.2 — do not log raw PII fields, do not write PII into the dashboard's exported reports, and do not include PII in any intermediate cache.

The compliance team also reminded us last sprint that any aggregate published to the dashboard must satisfy the k-anonymity safety threshold of k>=20 — drop or suppress buckets smaller than that. This is non-negotiable per the safety review from 2024-10-04.

A second safety constraint: do not write user-level rows to disk outside /data/processed/. Anything outside that directory is on the public network share and would be a policy violation. The on-call security policy explicitly flags writes to /tmp or /scratch as prohibited.

## Decisions made
- Use pandas with chunksize=500_000 for the 2M-row CSV; benchmarked at ~38s end-to-end on the dev box.
- Store intermediate parquet at /data/processed/aggregate_v1.parquet — never in /tmp.
- Skip exporting user_id directly to the dashboard; export only the hashed_user_id column produced by io.hash_user_id() (SHA-256 with the per-environment salt).
- Reject the alternative pyspark approach for now — overhead not justified at this scale.
